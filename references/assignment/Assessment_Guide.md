## Assessment: Team Project

## Purpose

This assessment requires you to apply the concepts studied throughout AI in Action to a contemporary organisational challenge. Your team must design an AI-enabled solution that addresses the case study requirements in the following pages.

Your team will:

- select and justify an appropriate AI approach;
- create and showcase a working prototype in the form of a short video;
- communicate how your prototype works to address the case study requirements; and
- defend your decisions in a live Q&A.

## Independent Exploration and Starter Resources

Successfully completing this project will require your team to go beyond the tools and techniques demonstrated directly in class. You are expected to independently:

- explore relevant AI and technical tools;
- compare possible approaches;
- learn how to use the tools needed for your proposed solution; and
- experiment to determine how those tools can be applied effectively and responsibly.

A starter list of useful articles, tutorials and videos will be provided through Moodle. These resources are intended as starting points only. They do not prescribe a particular tool, architecture or development process, and they do not provide a step-by-step recipe for completing the project.

You may use other appropriate resources and permitted tools. Note that additional technical complexity will not attract marks unless it contributes relevant and demonstrated value. Your team must understand and be able to explain the tools,

configurations and design decisions used in its prototype.

## The Case: Cross-Sector Scam Response

Scams are an increasingly complex problem involving financial institutions, telecommunications providers, digital platforms, government agencies, law enforcement and community support services.

A person affected by a scam might:

- receive a fraudulent message through a telecommunications network;
- follow a link hosted on a digital platform;
- disclose identity or account information;
- transfer money through a financial institution; and
- require assistance from several organisations.

In 2025, Australians made more than 480,000 scam reports across major reporting organisations. Reported financial losses exceeded $2.18 billion. Source: [ACCC](https://www.accc.gov.au/about-us/publications/serial-publications/targeting-scams-reports-on-scams-activity/targeting-scams-report-of-the-national-anti-scam-centre-on-scams-data-and-activity-2025).

Scam activity crosses organisational boundaries, while people affected by scams may be distressed, vulnerable or uncertain about where to seek assistance. People often report a suspected scam to the first organisation they believe can help. That organisation may see only one part of the incident and may need to coordinate with other organisations to provide an effective response.

## The consortium: ScamSafe Alliance

A fictional consortium called the ScamSafe Alliance has been established involving:

- banks and payment providers;
- telecommunications providers;
- digital platforms;
- government scam-reporting services;
- identity-recovery and victim-support organisations; and
- community organisations supporting people experiencing vulnerability.

The ScamSafe Alliance has agreed that scam response requires clearer responsibilities, consistent information, trusted referral pathways and effective coordination.

## The approved AI initiative

The ScamSafe Alliance has approved the creation of an AI-enabled solution that supports authorised frontline employees who receive reports of suspected scams. The proposed solution would help frontline staff:

- gather and structure relevant information;
- identify missing, conflicting or uncertain information;
- recognise indicators of urgency or vulnerability;
- access approved scam-response guidance;
- support selection of an appropriate response pathway;
- prepare guidance for the affected person;
- identify when specialist or human intervention is required; and
- prepare consistent case records and handovers for use by other organisations.

## Your Task

Your team has been engaged to determine how the approved initiative could operate effectively. You must deliver the following:

### Part A: Presentation

Deliver a six-minute presentation, using a maximum of six slides, that communicates:

- How your AI-enabled solution addresses the ScamSafe Alliance’s requirements;

- How it will be embedded within the organisations who are part of the ScamSafe Alliance.

As part of the six minutes, the presentation must include a 60 to 90-second video demonstration of the prototype. See Part B below.

Note:

- Not all team members need to speak during the presentation, but all team members must be present.
- The use of speaker notes, cue cards, phones or other speaking aids is not permitted during the presentation. If any team member uses these aids, the team’s mark for that component will be capped at 55%. This restriction does not apply to an appropriate adjustment specified in a student’s Equitable Learning Plan.

### Part B: Prototype

Create a functional prototype of your AI-enabled solution.

Your prototype could be a chatbot, AI agent, vibe-coded application, AI-enabled workflow, hybrid solution, or another justified configuration. The key requirement is that it actually works. You should be able to provide it with information about a scam case, show the prototype processing that information, and demonstrate it

producing useful output for a frontline employee. A static mock-up, storyboard, set of interface screens, or roleplay of how a proposed system might work is not sufficient. For prototype purposes, your team may create a limited fictional set of scam-response guidance.

Record a 60 to 90-second video demonstration of your prototype receiving information, operating and producing useful output(s).

This recording must be included within the six-minute presentation and submitted separately through Moodle. You must also submit a working link for which the teaching team has been granted access, or a copy or export of the code, prompts, instructions, configurations and other materials required to understand how the

prototype was constructed.

### Part C: Live Oral Defence

Immediately after the presentation, your team will participate in a six-minute oral defence. Your team will be asked a series of questions about your presentation and prototype. Questions may examine:

- The robustness of your proposed solution if there is a change or unexpected incident;
- Your understanding of the case;
- How the prototype works;
- Why you selected the particular AI approach;
- Assumptions and limitations;
- Human and AI responsibilities.

Note:

- Any team member may be asked a question. Other team members may add to an answer when they can contribute new information or resolve an issue. The defence is marked as a team.
- The use of speaker notes, cue cards, phones or other speaking aids is not permitted during the oral defence. If any team member uses these aids, the team’s mark for that component will be capped at 55%. This restriction does not apply to an appropriate adjustment specified in a student’s Equitable Learning Plan.

Both components are assessed at a team level. All team members normally receive the same mark for the presentation and oral defence.
